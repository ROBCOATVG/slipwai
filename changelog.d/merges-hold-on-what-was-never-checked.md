PATCH

**A merge is refused when the gate skipped part of itself.** The harbourmaster granted a merge on `make verify`'s exit code alone, and a gate that ran nothing still exits 0: every one of recap's slices with a screen merged over `render gate(s) SKIPPED, not passed`, for fifteen days, while 22 of its screens ran 91px off a phone. A gate whose output says `SKIPPED, not passed` is now refused, as something the berth can fix. The scratch worktree the merge is gated in also gets what extensions installed under `tools/` and Git ignores, so an installed kit is no longer *not installed in this checkout* there.

**A capability's last slice merges only once its gates have cleared.** The capability gates — converge, gaps, design review, adversary, mutation and the walk — were due *after* the last merge under every release mode but `keystone`, and nothing owned *after*: the captain dispatches slices, found none left, and exited. recap merged all 34 of its slices, the board said *Built*, and none of its eight capabilities was ever gated; when a person finally ran them on one, they found 34 findings, two HIGH. The harbourmaster now refuses the merge that would make a capability whole until its `gates.md` says `Cleared:` on that slice's branch, in every release mode, and `/sail` runs them there. The board says *Merged, not yet gated* rather than *Built* while a whole capability is owed its gates.

**A merge parks when trunk's CI cannot be read, unless `harbour.json` says `ci: off`.** A forge that could not be asked — no remote, `gh` missing or refused — read as *unverified*, and only red stopped a merge: recap had no remote for fifteen days and every merge was granted `ci=unverified` over a CI workflow that would have failed from its first commit. Such a merge is now refused for a person, naming why the forge could not be asked, and clears itself the first pass it can. A project with no CI says `"ci": "off"` and merges as before.

**And two that only a second checkout could see.** `check-decisions` refuses an absolute path, or one that climbs out of the project, in *Written to* or *Evidence* — recap's LB9 named its recordings by a path on the machine that walked it. The integration job's container checkout names its workspace `safe.directory`, which git otherwise refuses as dubious ownership when the container runs as root.

**Catch-up:** `slipwai migrate` brings the toolkit's harbourmaster, `capabilities.py` and `check-decisions.py` to an existing project. A project with no remote adds one, or sets `"ci": "off"` in `harbour.json`, before its next merge.
